# Private AI motion video

A single-file animated explainer (about 76 seconds) of the setup: Ollama with `qwen3.5:9b` on a MacBook Air, Tailscale between devices, and the Enchanted app on iPhone. It has a built-in soft ambient music track, a spoken narration, and no dependencies or network needed.

## Open it

On a Mac, from the project folder:

```
open video/index.html
```

Or serve it locally:

```
cd video
python3 -m http.server 8000
```

then visit http://localhost:8000 in Safari or Chrome.

Click the video or press **Space** to play and pause. Use the slider to scrub, **Restart** to start over, and the left and right arrow keys to jump 5 seconds. The stage scales to the window; use full screen (green button or Ctrl+Cmd+F) for best results.

## Sound

The music is generated by the page itself with the Web Audio API (pads, a gentle pluck arpeggio, bass, a light hi-hat, and a small chime at each scene change). There is no audio file and nothing to download, so it works from `file://` and in htmlpreview.

- Audio starts when you click play (browsers require a click first). The first play takes a moment to build the track, shown as "Loading audio..." on the button; then the music joins at the current position.
- Pause, restart, the slider and the arrow keys all keep the music in sync with the picture.
- **Mute** button (or press **M**) and the volume slider are in the bottom bar.
- **Audio .wav** downloads the music track (76 seconds) so you can add it to a recording.

## Narration

The page reads the script aloud with your browser's built-in text to speech (the Web Speech API, `speechSynthesis`). Nothing is downloaded and no account or API key is needed, so it works from `file://` and in htmlpreview.

- It starts when you click play. Each of the 24 lines is spoken at its start time, matching the captions. Pause, restart, the slider and the arrow keys keep the voice in step: speech stops on pause, and after a jump the current line is spoken if it has only just begun, otherwise the voice waits for the next line.
- **Voice: on/off** button (or press **N**) switches the narration. The choice is remembered.
- The voice drop-down lists the voices on your device, English first, with natural ones (Samantha, Ava, Allison, Siri, Enhanced, Premium, Google US English) at the top. Your pick is remembered. The small slider next to it sets the narration volume.
- The music dips automatically while the voice is speaking and comes back afterwards. **Mute** and the music volume slider still work.
- The spoken text is separate from the captions so names sound right (for example "Oh-lama" for Ollama). Long addresses are left to the captions.

Voices vary by browser and system. On a Mac, for a much nicer voice: System Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, System Voice, Manage Voices, then download an Enhanced, Premium or Siri voice and reload the page. Firefox and Linux may have no voices at all, in which case the button shows "No voice" and the video plays with music only.

Limitations: the browser speaks through the system, so the narration is not in the **Audio .wav** download and a Cmd+Shift+5 screen recording will not capture it. Speech timing depends on the voice; lines are kept short and the speed is adjusted slightly (0.95x to 1.15x) so each fits before the next one.

## Export with narration

Click **Script** in the bottom bar for three downloads:

- `narration-script.txt`: the spoken lines with timestamps.
- `private-ai-video.srt`: subtitles, for iMovie or a video editor.
- `make-narration.sh`: a Mac script that records each line with the macOS `say` voice (Samantha by default), places it at the right time with ffmpeg, and writes `narration.wav`.

Steps:

1. `brew install ffmpeg` (once).
2. Record the picture as described above, and click **Audio .wav** to get `private-ai-video-music.wav`.
3. Put the recording, the music wav and `make-narration.sh` in one folder, then run:
   `chmod +x make-narration.sh && ./make-narration.sh recording.mov`
   This writes `narration.wav` and `out.mp4` (picture, narration, and the music at a low level).
   Pick another voice or speed with `VOICE=Ava RATE=175 ./make-narration.sh recording.mov` (list voices with `say -v '?'`). Check that no line runs into the next; lower RATE value is slower, higher is faster.

Or skip ffmpeg: import the recording, the music wav and `narration.wav` (or the `.srt`) into iMovie and line them up at the start.

## Record it as an mp4

1. Open the page and make the window large or full screen. Pause at the start.
2. Press **Cmd+Shift+5** and choose "Record Selected Portion" (or "Record Entire Screen").
3. Click Record, then press Space to start playback.
4. When it ends (about 1 minute 16 seconds), click the stop button in the menu bar.

The recording is saved as a `.mov` on your Desktop. QuickTime Player can export it: File, Export As. To get mp4 with ffmpeg: `ffmpeg -i recording.mov -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p out.mp4`.

## Export with sound

Screen recording with Cmd+Shift+5 does not capture the computer's sound unless you add a virtual audio device, so the easiest way is to add the music afterwards:

1. Record the picture as above (the recording will be silent, which is fine).
2. Click **Audio .wav** on the page to download `private-ai-video-music.wav`.
3. Combine them. With ffmpeg:
   `ffmpeg -i recording.mov -i private-ai-video-music.wav -c:v libx264 -pix_fmt yuv420p -c:a aac -shortest out.mp4`
   Or without ffmpeg, drag both files into iMovie, line the start of the music up with the start of the video, and export.

Start the music and the recording at the same moment if you need exact sync: trim the start of the recording to the instant the title appears.
